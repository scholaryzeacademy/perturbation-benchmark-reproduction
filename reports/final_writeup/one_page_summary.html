Norman (2019) & Adamson (2016) Perturb-seq datasets
2026-07-31

An independent, transparent reproduction of an unresolved 2025 debate: does deep learning beat simple linear baselines at predicting post-perturbation gene expression, or is that conclusion an artifact of uncalibrated metrics? Three baselines, GEARS, and scGPT were benchmarked head-to-head on both datasets under conventional metrics and a calibration framework that scores each model against the gap between a no-signal negative control and a best-achievable positive control.

Scope note. The positive control is built from repeated random 50/50 splits of each real test condition's own held-out cells (this dataset has no true biological-replicate metadata) — a documented narrowing of the calibration method, not a full reproduction of the rebuttal paper's 13-metric framework. Geneformer (zero-shot) outputs a per-cell cosine-shift ranking, not a predicted expression profile, so it is excluded from the table above and reported in its own ranking table in the full write-up.
